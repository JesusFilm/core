## Query

> My boyfriend and I have been sleeping together. We're planning to get married next year. Is that really a problem if we're committed to each other?

## Output

> I hear you — you're committed to each other and marriage is on the horizon. That matters. But yes, premarital sexual intimacy is a real problem from God's perspective, and not because the commitment doesn't count or because your love isn't genuine.
>
> Scripture is clear on this. Paul writes, "Flee from sexual immorality. Every other sin a person commits is outside the body, but the sexually immoral person sins against his own body" (1 Corinthians 6:18). He goes on to say that your body is "a temple of the Holy Spirit" (1 Corinthians 6:19). Sexual union is designed by God for marriage — it binds two people together in a way that reflects Christ's covenant with the church (Ephesians 5:31–32). Outside that covenant, it separates you from God's design for your sexuality and your relationship.
>
> The problem isn't that you don't love each other or that marriage will happen eventually. The problem is that you're treating as common what God has set apart as sacred. That offends His holiness and breaks His design for your good, not His harm.
>
> Here's the harder part: continuing this now won't make the sin smaller by next year. Repentance means stopping — turning away from what you know grieves God and asking His forgiveness. You can do that today. It will be costly, yes. But God's grace covers real repentance, and choosing to honor Him now, before marriage, will mean something profound about who you're each choosing to become.
>
> What would it look like for you to make a change here? That's the question worth sitting with.
