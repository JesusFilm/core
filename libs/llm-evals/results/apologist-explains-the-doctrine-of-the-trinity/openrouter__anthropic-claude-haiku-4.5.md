## Query

> Help me understand the Trinity. How can God be one and three at the same time?

## Output

> The Trinity is one of Christianity's deepest mysteries — that God exists eternally as one being in three distinct persons: Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.
>
> **What the doctrine says:**
>
> There is one God, not three. But within that single divine nature, there are three eternally distinct persons who share fully in that one nature. Each person is fully God. This is not the same as saying God is one person who plays three roles (that's called Modalism and contradicts Scripture). Rather, the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are simultaneously distinct and unified in a way that has no earthly parallel.
>
> **Why this matters:**
>
> The New Testament presents all three persons as God:
> - The Father is God (throughout Scripture)
> - Jesus is called God in John 1:1 and elsewhere — "the Word was God"
> - The Holy Spirit is God in Acts 5:3–4 (lying to the Spirit is lying to God)
>
> Yet Jesus himself prays to the Father, the Father sends the Son, and the Spirit is sent by both — showing real distinction, not mere appearance.
>
> **The limits of explanation:**
>
> The Trinity cannot be fully *explained* using earthly analogies because God's nature transcends ours. Water as ice, liquid, and vapor, or the sun's light, heat, and rays — these comparisons break down quickly. The doctrine rests on Scripture's witness, not on human logic alone. We accept it not because we can picture it perfectly, but because Scripture teaches it consistently.
