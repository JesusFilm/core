## Output

> The doctrine of the Trinity means that there is only one God, who eternally exists as three distinct persons: the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit.
>
> To understand how God can be both one and three without contradiction, it helps to distinguish between **being** (*what* God is) and **person** (*who* God is).
>
> ### 1. One in Essence (*What* God Is)
> The Bible teaches strict monotheism. God is not three separate gods (which would be polytheism or tritheism). There is only one divine nature, essence, or substance.
>
> > "Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God, the LORD is one." (Deuteronomy 6:4, ESV)
>
> ### 2. Three in Person (*Who* God Is)
> Within the one divine essence, there are three eternal, distinct persons: the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. They are not three modes or "masks" that God puts on (a heresy known as modalism), but three distinct centers of consciousness in eternal relationship with one another.
>
> - The Father is fully God (Philippians 2:11).
> - The Son is fully God (John 1:1, Colossians 2:9).
> - The Holy Spirit is fully God (Acts 5:3–4).
>
> A clear example of this distinction is Jesus' baptism, where the Son is baptized in the water, the Spirit descends like a dove, and the Father speaks from heaven (Matthew 3:16–17).
>
> ### 3. Avoiding the Contradiction
> A contradiction occurs only if we claim God is one in the *same way* He is three. Christianity does not teach that God is one person and three persons, nor that He is one essence and three essences. Rather:
>
> - God is **one** in **essence** (substance).
> - God is **three** in **person** (relational identity).
>
> Each person possesses the entire divine nature equally and eternally, yet each relates to the others in distinct ways.
>
