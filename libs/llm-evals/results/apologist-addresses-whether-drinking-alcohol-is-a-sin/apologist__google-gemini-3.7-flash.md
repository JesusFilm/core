## Output

>
> - **Drunkenness is strictly forbidden:** While moderate drinking is permitted, losing control and becoming intoxicated is explicitly identified as sin. As Scripture commands, *"And do not get drunk with wine, for that is debauchery, but be filled with the Spirit"* (Ephesians 5:18; see also Proverbs 20:1, Galatians 5:19–21).
> - **Wine can be received with thanksgiving:** Scripture often references wine as a part of normal life and even a blessing when used rightly (Psalm 104:14–15). Jesus' first miracle was turning water into wine at a wedding feast (John 2:1–11), and Paul advised Timothy to drink a little wine for his health (1 Timothy 5:23).
> - **Conscience and wisdom matter:** If drinking alcohol causes you to stumble, tempts you toward excess, or troubles your conscience, it is wise to abstain. As Romans 14:23 notes, *"whatever does not proceed from faith is sin."* Believers are also called to avoid causing others who may struggle with addiction to stumble (Romans 14:21).
>
> If you can enjoy a glass of wine in moderation, maintaining complete sobriety and a clear conscience with thanksgiving to God, you have the Christian freedom to do so. If you find it weakens your self-control or violates your personal conviction, laying it aside is a good and honorable choice.
